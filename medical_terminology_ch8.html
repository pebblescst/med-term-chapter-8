Internal Female Reproductive Anatomy
1
Ovariesoophor/o, ovari/o
Paired almond-shaped female gonads in the pelvic cavity. Two functions: (1) Produce ova (oogenesis); (2) Secrete estrogen and progesterone. Contain follicles at various stages of development. Corpus luteum forms after ovulation — secretes progesterone.
2
Fallopian Tubes (Uterine Tubes)salping/o
Two tubes (~10 cm) extending from uterus toward ovaries. Fimbriae (finger-like projections) sweep ovum into tube. Site of fertilization. Cilia and peristalsis move fertilized ovum toward uterus (~3–4 days).
3
Uterushyster/o, metr/o, metri/o
Hollow pear-shaped muscular organ. Three layers: Perimetrium (outer serosal layer), Myometrium (metr/o — thick muscular middle layer; contracts during labor), Endometrium (endometri/o — inner mucosal layer that sheds during menstruation / implants embryo).
4
Cervixcervic/o
Lower narrow portion of the uterus; opens into the vagina. Contains the cervical canal and cervical os. Pap smear samples cervical cells for cancer screening. Dilates during labor (0–10 cm).
5
Vaginavagin/o, colp/o
Muscular canal (~9 cm) from cervix to exterior; birth canal during delivery; receives penis during coitus. Bartholin glands provide lubrication. Hymen = thin membrane partially covering vaginal opening.
6
Vulva & External Genitaliavulv/o, episi/o
Collective external female genitalia. Includes: Mons pubis (fatty mound), Labia majora (outer folds), Labia minora (inner folds), Clitoris (erectile tissue; sensory), Vestibule (area surrounding vaginal/urethral openings), Perineum (area between vagina and anus). Culdocentesis (culd/o) = aspiration from rectouterine pouch.

The Menstrual Cycle (~28 days)
The menstrual cycle is regulated by the hypothalamus-pituitary-ovarian axis. Key hormones: FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone), LH (luteinizing hormone), Estrogen, Progesterone.
1
Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5)
Endometrium sheds (menstruation/men/o). Estrogen and progesterone drop → uterine lining sloughs off. Menstrual flow = blood + endometrial tissue. Average blood loss: ~30–80 mL.
2
Follicular Phase (Days 1–13)
FSH stimulates follicle development in the ovary. Rising estrogen thickens endometrium (proliferative phase). One dominant follicle develops → Graafian follicle.
3
Ovulation (Day 14)
LH surge triggers release of the ovum (ovulation). Ovum swept into fallopian tube by fimbriae. Fertile window: ~5 days before and 1 day after ovulation.
4
Luteal Phase (Days 14–28)
Corpus luteum (ruptured follicle) secretes progesterone → maintains endometrium for implantation. If no fertilization: corpus luteum degenerates → progesterone falls → menstruation begins. If fertilization: hCG maintains corpus luteum.
Key Hormones
Estrogen — stimulates endometrial growth; secondary sex characteristics; produced by follicles and corpus luteum
Progesterone — maintains endometrium for implantation; produced by corpus luteum; "pro-gestation"
FSH — stimulates follicle development
LH — triggers ovulation; stimulates corpus luteum
hCG — human chorionic gonadotropin; produced by embryo after implantation; detected by pregnancy tests

Nullipara — nulli- + -para; never delivered
Primigravida — primi- + -gravida; first pregnancy
Multigravida — multi- + -gravida; multiple pregnancies
Gestation — ~40 weeks (280 days) from LMP
Antenatal / Prenatal — before birth (ante- / pre-)
Postpartum — after delivery
Fetal & placental structures

Labor & Delivery Terms
Parturition
part/o; act of giving birth; labor and delivery
Cesarean section (CS)
Delivery through abdominal and uterine incision; when vaginal delivery is unsafe
Episiotomy
episi/o + -tomy; incision of the perineum to widen vaginal opening during delivery
-tocia
Suffix meaning labor/birth condition; dystocia = difficult labor; eutocia = normal labor
Puerperium
puerper/o; 6-week period following delivery during which uterus returns to pre-pregnancy size (involution)
Erythroblastosis fetalis
Rh incompatibility; Rh-negative mother produces antibodies against Rh-positive fetal blood → hemolytic disease of newborn; prevented by RhoGAM
Oligohydramnios
olig/o (few) + hydr/o (water) + amni/o; abnormally low amniotic fluid volume
Nat/o terms
nat/o = birth; neonatal (neo- + nat/o = newborn), antenatal (ante- + nat/o = before birth), postnatal (post- + nat/o = after birth)
Endometriosis
Endometrial tissue grows outside the uterus (ovaries, tubes, peritoneum). endometri/o + -osis. Responds to hormonal cycle → pain, dysmenorrhea, infertility. Treatment: hormones, laparoscopic excision.
Leiomyoma (fibroid)
Benign smooth muscle tumor of the myometrium. leio- (smooth) + my/o + -oma. Most common benign uterine tumor. Symptoms: menorrhagia, pelvic pressure, dysmenorrhea. Treatment: UAE, myomectomy, hysterectomy.
Hysterosalpingography (HSG)
hyster/o + salping/o + -graphy. X-ray of uterus and fallopian tubes after contrast injection; diagnoses tubal obstruction, uterine defects; used in infertility workup.
Hysteroptosis
Prolapse/downward displacement of the uterus. hyster/o + -ptosis (drooping). Uterus descends into vaginal canal; from weakened pelvic floor. Treatment: pessary or hysteropexy.
Hysteropexy
Surgical fixation of the uterus. hyster/o + -pexy. Treats uterine prolapse (hysteroptosis).
Endometrial carcinoma
Most common gynecologic malignancy; arises from endometrium; risk: estrogen excess, obesity, nulliparity; classic symptom = postmenopausal bleeding.
Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
poly- (many) + cyst + ovary; multiple small ovarian cysts from follicle arrest; most common endocrine disorder in reproductive-age females. Features: irregular periods, anovulation, hyperandrogenism, insulin resistance. Treatment: lifestyle, OCPs, metformin.
Ovarian cyst
Fluid-filled sac in/on the ovary; most are functional (follicular or corpus luteum cysts) and resolve spontaneously; dermoid cysts, endometriomas may need treatment.
Ovarian carcinoma
Most lethal gynecologic cancer; often detected late (silent); elevated CA-125; risk: BRCA mutation, family history. Treatment: debulking surgery + chemotherapy.
Salpingitis
Inflammation of the fallopian tube. salping/o + -itis. Often from STI (chlamydia, gonorrhea); can cause pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) and tubal scarring → infertility, ectopic pregnancy.
Salpingolysis
salping/o + -lysis. Surgical breaking/dissolving of fallopian tube adhesions; treats tubal obstruction from PID or endometriosis.
Oophorectomy
oophor/o + -ectomy. Surgical removal of an ovary; for cancer, large cysts, endometriosis. Bilateral oophorectomy = surgical menopause.
Anovulation
an- (without) + ovulation; failure to release an ovum; common in PCOS; leading cause of female infertility.
Cervical dysplasia
Abnormal cell growth in the cervix. cervic/o + dys- + -plasia. CIN (cervical intraepithelial neoplasia); caused primarily by HPV; detected by Pap smear; can progress to cervical carcinoma if untreated.
Cervical carcinoma
Most commonly squamous cell carcinoma; almost entirely caused by HPV (types 16 & 18); preventable by HPV vaccine; detected by Pap smear + HPV co-test. Treatment: surgery, radiation, chemotherapy.
Cervicitis
Inflammation of the cervix. cervic/o + -itis. Usually from STI (chlamydia, gonorrhea, herpes); purulent discharge, bleeding.
Vulvovaginitis
Inflammation of the vulva and vagina. vulv/o + vagin/o + -itis. Causes: bacterial vaginosis (BV), candidiasis (yeast), Trichomonas; symptoms: discharge, itching, odor.
Colposcopy
colp/o + -scopy. Visual examination of the vagina and cervix with a magnifying scope; performed after abnormal Pap smear; allows directed biopsy.
Culdocentesis
culd/o + -centesis. Aspiration of fluid from the rectouterine (cul-de-sac) pouch via vaginal wall; diagnoses ectopic pregnancy rupture or PID.
Ectopic pregnancy
Implantation of fertilized ovum OUTSIDE the uterus; most common in fallopian tube (tubal pregnancy). Rupture = life-threatening hemorrhage. Treatment: methotrexate or surgery.
Preeclampsia
Hypertension + proteinuria + edema after 20 weeks gestation; can progress to eclampsia (seizures); requires delivery. Risk: first pregnancy, obesity, multiple gestation.
Placenta previa
Placenta covers cervical os (opening); painless bright red vaginal bleeding in 3rd trimester; delivery by cesarean section.
Erythroblastosis fetalis
Rh incompatibility — Rh(-) mother sensitized to Rh(+) fetal blood cells → maternal antibodies cross placenta → fetal hemolysis. Prevented by RhoGAM (Rh immunoglobulin) at 28 weeks and delivery.
Choriocarcinoma
chori/o + carcin/o + -oma. Malignant gestational trophoblastic tumor arising from chorionic tissue; very sensitive to chemotherapy; highly curable.
AFP test
Alpha-fetoprotein screening test; elevated in open neural tube defects (spina bifida); decreased in Down syndrome; part of maternal serum screening.
Amniocentesis
amni/o + -centesis. Aspiration of amniotic fluid for genetic/chromosomal testing; performed at 15–20 weeks; diagnoses Down syndrome, genetic disorders, lung maturity.
Fibrocystic breast disease
Most common benign breast condition; lumpy, tender breasts that worsen before menstruation; related to hormonal fluctuations; benign but may require biopsy to exclude cancer.
Fibroadenoma
Most common benign breast tumor in young women; firm, smooth, mobile, non-tender mass; no malignant potential; often observed or excised.
Breast carcinoma
Most common cancer in women; majority are invasive ductal carcinoma; risk factors: BRCA1/2, family history, age, estrogen exposure. Screening: mammography (mamm/o + -graphy).
Mastopexy
mast/o + -pexy. Surgical fixation/lifting of the breast; cosmetic/reconstructive procedure.
Theleplasty
thel/e + -plasty. Surgical repair/reconstruction of the nipple.
Mammography
mamm/o + -graphy. X-ray imaging of breast tissue; primary screening tool for breast cancer; recommended annually from age 40–50 depending on guidelines.
Diagnostic Procedures
Pap smear (Papanicolaou test)
Cervical cell sampling; screens for cervical dysplasia and cancer; combined with HPV co-test; performed during pelvic exam
Colposcopy (colp/o + -scopy)
Magnified visual exam of vagina and cervix after abnormal Pap; allows targeted biopsy of suspicious areas
Hysterosalpingography (HSG)
hyster/o + salping/o + -graphy; X-ray with contrast dye through uterus/tubes; infertility workup for tubal patency
Culdocentesis (culd/o + -centesis)
Needle aspiration of fluid from rectouterine pouch; diagnoses ectopic pregnancy rupture, PID
Amniocentesis (amni/o + -centesis)
Aspiration of amniotic fluid; genetic testing for chromosomal abnormalities at 15–20 weeks
Mammography (mamm/o + -graphy)
Breast X-ray; primary cancer screening tool; detects tumors before palpable
AFP test
Alpha-fetoprotein; maternal serum screening for neural tube defects and chromosomal abnormalities
Pelvic ultrasound
Imaging of uterus, ovaries, pregnancy; transvaginal (TV) for better resolution; no radiation; monitors fetal development
Therapeutic Procedures
Uterus & tubes
Hysterectomy (hyster/o + -ectomy)
Surgical removal of uterus; for fibroids, cancer, prolapse; total = uterus + cervix; radical = + parametrium
Hysteropexy (hyster/o + -pexy)
Surgical fixation of a prolapsed uterus; treats hysteroptosis
Tubal ligation
Permanent female sterilization; fallopian tubes cut, tied, burned, or clipped to prevent fertilization
Salpingolysis (salping/o + -lysis)
Breaking down/releasing fallopian tube adhesions; treats PID scarring and infertility
UAE — Uterine artery embolization
Minimally invasive; blocks blood supply to uterine fibroids → shrinkage; uterus-sparing alternative to hysterectomy
Episiotomy (episi/o + -tomy)
Incision of perineum to enlarge vaginal opening during delivery; prevents uncontrolled tearing
Ovaries
Oophorectomy (oophor/o + -ectomy)
Surgical removal of an ovary; bilateral = surgical menopause; for cancer, large cysts
Breast
Mastopexy (mast/o + -pexy)
Surgical breast lift/fixation
Mastectomy (mast/o + -ectomy)
Surgical removal of breast; for cancer; radical = breast + lymph nodes + pectoral muscles
